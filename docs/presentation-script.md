# Presentation Script — Smart Crop Advisory System for Small & Marginal Farmers
**Speakers: Milani Sonpipre & Paras Rajak**
**Duration: ~3 minutes (adjust pacing as needed)**

---

**[MILANI — opens with a question, not a definition]**

Milani: Quick question for everyone in the room — who do you think an Indian farmer trusts more for advice on their crop: a government advisory office, or their neighbor?

*(pause for 2 seconds)*

Milani: Most of the time — it's the neighbor. Not because the neighbor knows more. But because expert advice is slow to reach them, and for a small or marginal farmer — someone farming maybe one or two acres — there's no dedicated agronomist checking in on their field. Whatever advice comes, comes late, or comes secondhand.

---

**[PARAS — takes over with the "why now"]**

Paras: And for a small farmer, that delay isn't a minor inconvenience — it's the difference between a profitable season and a loss they can't recover from. They don't have the buffer that a large farm does. One missed disease warning, one wrong irrigation decision, one ignored weather alert — and the entire season's income is at risk.

Paras: The information they need — what to plant, when to water, how to treat a disease, when a scheme deadline is approaching — already exists somewhere. It's just scattered, delayed, or written in language and formats that don't reach them in time.

---

**[MILANI — introduces the solution]**

Milani: So that's exactly the gap we're closing — a Smart Crop Advisory System, built specifically around the small and marginal farmer, not the large commercial one.

Milani: At its core, the farmer interacts with one simple app. They can snap a photo of a diseased leaf and get an instant diagnosis. They get weather-based alerts before a risk becomes a loss. And they get all of it in their own language, in plain, actionable terms — not technical reports.

---

**[PARAS — walks through the flow, concretely]**

Paras: Let's walk through how it actually works, in three parts.

Paras: Part one — diagnosis. The farmer photographs a leaf, and our AI model — trained to recognize common crop diseases — identifies the problem in seconds and suggests the right treatment.

Paras: Part two — advisory. Beyond just disease, the system pulls in weather forecasts and soil conditions to proactively tell the farmer what to do next — irrigate, hold off, apply treatment now before the rain makes it worse.

Paras: Part three — access. Everything is designed to reach the farmer however they can be reached — a smartphone app where possible, and SMS or a basic voice call where a smartphone or stable internet isn't an option.

---

**[MILANI — addresses the obvious question: "why is this different"]**

Milani: Now, advisory apps for farmers already exist. So let's be clear about what's different in our approach.

Milani: First — we're not designing for the average farmer. We're designing specifically for the small and marginal farmer — someone with limited land, limited resources, and very little margin for error. Every design choice, from icon-first navigation to offline access, comes back to that constraint.

Milani: Second — this isn't just a disease-lookup tool. It's a full advisory loop — disease, weather, soil, and government scheme information, connected together, instead of scattered across five different sources the farmer would never realistically check.

Milani: And third — we're building for genuinely low-connectivity access from day one, not as an afterthought bolted on later.

---

**[PARAS — closes with impact + call to action]**

Paras: At the core, small and marginal farmers make up the overwhelming majority of India's farming population — and they're also the ones with the least access to timely expert advice. That's not a small niche problem. That's the actual center of Indian agriculture.

Paras: A photo, a forecast, a language they understand, delivered however they can receive it — that's the entire experience we're building around them.

Paras: We'd love to walk you through the live demo now — it'll make a lot more sense once you see how a real farmer's real question gets a real answer, in seconds.

**[Transition to live demo]**

---

## Delivery Notes
- Keep the pause after Milani's opening question — it's doing the work of hooking the judges, don't rush past it.
- When Paras says "small and marginal farmers make up the overwhelming majority" — say it with weight, that line is your strongest impact statement, don't rush through it.
- Paras's three-part walkthrough should be said while pointing at the actual screen/slide, narrating the action rather than reciting a list.
- Avoid a singsong "presentation voice" — talk like you're explaining something you're genuinely excited about, not reciting memorized lines.
- If a judge interrupts with a question mid-script, don't try to get back "on script" word for word — just pick the thread back up naturally.
